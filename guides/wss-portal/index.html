WSS Portal: Which Web Self Service Is Yours?

7 min read · Updated 11 August 2026 · TheDiscomBill Editorial · How we source our rates

WSS stands for Web Self Service, and several Indian DISCOMs use that name for their consumer portal — UPPCL in Uttar Pradesh, MSEDCL (Mahavitaran) in Maharashtra, KSEB in Kerala, UPCL in Uttarakhand and JPDCL in the Jammu division of Jammu & Kashmir. They are separate sites with separate logins, which is why searching for "WSS portal" on its own tends to land you on the wrong one — and why UPCL and UPPCL are especially easy to mix up: one letter apart, neighbouring states, both running a WSS. This page sorts out which is yours in about a minute, and tells you what to search for if your DISCOM is not one of the five.

UPPCL WSS: every service, with the direct link

Uttar Pradesh has the largest WSS audience of the five. One portal at consumer.uppcl.org/wss covers all five UP distribution companies — DVVNL, MVVNL, PUVVNL, PVVNL and KESCo — so there is no separate site per DISCOM. Paying a bill needs no account; everything in the table below does.

Every link in that table needs you to be signed in. Opened while logged out they bounce to the UPPCL login, which asks for your district and sends an OTP — so sign in first and the links land where they should. If you have no account yet, start at New Registration.

The one thing this portal will not do is reduce your load. Enhancement is a form you can file in a couple of minutes; reduction is not offered online at all and still means a written application at your sub-division office. If UPPCL raised your load automatically after a smart meter recorded your demand, read why sanctioned load suddenly increases — the portal still helps there, just with the evidence rather than the fix: pull your billing history to see the demand pattern, then file a Bill Correction Request or a Meter Related Complaint if the revision came from a single spike or a meter you doubt.

MSEDCL WSS: pay without an account

Maharashtra's portal is the most permissive of the five. You can pay a bill without registering at all: choose Pay Bill, type the 12-digit consumer number printed at the top of your Mahavitaran bill, check the units and due date, and pay by UPI, net banking or card. Registering is only worth it if you manage several connections or want your billing history in one place.

One quirk to know before you go looking: MSEDCL splits its services across two hosts. Bills, payments, readings and complaints live on wss.mahadiscom.in; the application-type services — load change, name change, refunds — live on css.mahadiscom.in, the Consumer Self Service site. Both are official, and the WSS home page links across to the second, so a service missing from one is usually on the other.

KSEB WSS: one portal for the whole state

Kerala has a single distributor, so one WSS covers everything: new connections, bills for an existing consumer number, ownership transfers, load changes and tariff changes. Less of it needs a login than you might expect — Quick Pay and complaint registration both work without an account, and registering mainly buys you billing history and alerts. A new LT connection costs a flat ₹50 to apply for, and small loads that need nothing more than a service drop can be energised in as little as two working days under KSEB's package connection.

UPCL WSS: Uttarakhand's single portal

Uttarakhand has one distribution licensee, Uttarakhand Power Corporation Limited, supplying around 2.6 million consumers across the state, and its consumer portal is branded WSS in so many words — the page itself is titled UPCL | WSS - Consumer Self Service. It sits at upcl.org/wss/login, reached from the main upcl.org site, and there is a matching UPCL Consumer Self Service app for Android and iOS.

A good deal of it is open without an account — paying a bill, both calculators, and every status check below need no login at all.

JPDCL WSS: the Jammu division only

Jammu Power Distribution Corporation Limited runs its consumer portal at a WSS address — wss.jpdcl.co.in — where you can view a bill assessment, look at historical meter readings, pay dues and file service requests such as a name change or a load enhancement. Registration uses your consumer code and account number with a mobile number or email.

There is no table of direct links for JPDCL here, and that is the portal's doing rather than an omission: unlike the other four it publishes no public service pages at all. Everything sits behind the sign-in, so the only honest links are Login, the sign-up beside it, and its FAQ and contact page. By its own description the portal covers viewing and downloading bills, payment and consumption history, updating consumer details, and managing service requests — but you will not see any of it until you have registered.

This is the Jammu division only. The Kashmir division is served by a different company, KPDCL, whose billing portal is not WSS-branded and lives at a separate address entirely — so a Srinagar consumer looking for "WSS" is looking for the wrong thing. Jammu rates are on our JPDCL tariff and bill calculator.

If your DISCOM is not one of the five

Most Indian DISCOMs run the same kind of self-service site but do not call it WSS. Searching for "WSS" plus their name will usually fail, because the term simply is not theirs — search for the DISCOM's own name plus "bill payment" or "consumer portal" instead, or start from our DISCOM services directory, which lists the official portal for each one.

Two things worth knowing before you go hunting. Some portals that once used a WSS address have since moved: Rajasthan's discoms were reachable at a wss1.rajdiscoms.com address that no longer resolves, so old links and screenshots pointing there are dead. And a similar-looking acronym is not the same service — "Consumer Self Service" or "CSS" portals do the same job under a different label. Follow the link printed on your own bill in preference to any of them.

What a WSS portal can and cannot do

All four cover the same ground: view the current bill, pay it, download past bills and receipts, and log service requests such as a name change or a load change. What neither will do is tell you whether the bill is correct. The portal shows you the amount the DISCOM has computed; it does not show the slab arithmetic behind it.

That is worth checking yourself when a bill jumps. Put your units into the bill calculator and compare the total, or read why an electricity bill suddenly increases for the usual causes.

Payments that do not show up straight away

A WSS payment is not always reflected on the bill immediately. Settlement can take a few hours, and in some cases until the next billing cycle, so keep the transaction reference until the portal shows the bill as paid. Do not pay twice on the strength of a stale screen. If a payment has left your account but the bill still reads unpaid after a working day, raise it with the DISCOM quoting the transaction ID rather than paying again.
